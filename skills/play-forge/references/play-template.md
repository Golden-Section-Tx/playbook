The opening. One to four paragraphs making the case for why this matters, in the second person, to a founder who is busy. Not a definition of the term. The paragraph that earns the play is usually the one that says what goes wrong when nobody does this — the thing you only know from having watched it.

Keep sentences short. Cut adjectives that are not working. No throat-clearing, no landscape-setting opener, no closing paragraph that restates the opening.

> **The goal:** Create the specific artifact this play produces, and the process that keeps it current.

#### Background

Optional, and used by roughly half the corpus. This is where a framework, a set of options, or the industry context goes when the steps would otherwise need a lecture inside them. If the steps can carry it, skip this section.

1. Option or concept one, explained in a sentence or two.
2. Option or concept two.
3. Option or concept three.

#### Steps

1. First action. Plain prose, imperative, addressed to the person doing it. Say what to gather, who to get in the room, and what the output of the step is. Sub-points are indented four spaces:
    - A question to answer in this step.
    - Another question, with the reason it matters.
2. Second action. Where a step needs a bolded label because the sequence has named phases, use `**Prepare:**`, `**Draft 1:**`, `**Feedback:**` — but most plays do not.
3. Feedback. Who reads the draft, what you ask them, and how to weigh what comes back.
4. Codification. Where the artifact lives, in what format, and who can change it.
5. Iteration cycle. Who owns it going forward, at what cadence, and what they look at each time. Almost every play ends here — a play with no recurrence step is a project, not a play.

#### Notes

Optional. Hard-won practices that do not fit the step sequence: pricing psychology, what to do at scale, what breaks first.

#### Troubleshooting

Optional, and used by about one play in seven. Two or three failure modes, each stated in the founder's own voice and then answered plainly.

*My numbers say I only need half the capital everyone else needed.* Possible, but unlikely — what proof do you have of top-decile performance? Go back and run the play again.

*I don't have the role this play assumes.* Say what to do instead, honestly, and what it costs to run the play without that person.
